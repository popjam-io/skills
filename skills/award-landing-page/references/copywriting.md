# Landing Page Copywriting: Frameworks and Worked Examples

## The copy document

Write the whole page as a markdown doc before touching HTML: every headline, every subhead, every CTA label, every quote. Design then gives each line the typographic weight its role deserves.

## Headline formulas (hero)

The hero headline must let a cold visitor answer "what is this and is it for me?" in under 5 seconds. Formulas that work, roughly in order of reliability:

1. **Outcome + timeframe/qualifier** — "From 400 support tickets to a ranked roadmap in 4 minutes."
2. **Point of view / enemy** — "Your roadmap shouldn't be a guess." (names the villain the audience already resents)
3. **Category + differentiator** — "The tax advisor built for Berlin freelancers."
4. **Customer's words** — lift a real quote's phrasing: "Finally, feedback that sorts itself."

Pair with a subhead that does the literal explaining (the headline earns attention; the subhead earns comprehension): *what it is, who it's for, the mechanism*.

**Weak → strong:**
- "Supercharge your product decisions" → "Stop guessing what to build next"
- "AI-powered feedback analysis" → "Reads every ticket, review, and call — hands you the top 10 things to fix"
- "Premium espresso, redefined" → "Café-grade espresso, 90 seconds after you wake up"

## Section patterns

- **Outcome section:** paint the after-state concretely. Show the artifact the product produces (the roadmap, the report, the shot of espresso), not abstract benefit prose.
- **How-it-works:** exactly 3–4 steps, each one verb-led ("Connect your helpdesk → We cluster every ticket → You ship what matters"). Steps that need paragraphs mean the product story is too complicated — simplify the story, not the reader.
- **Social proof:** specific beats prestigious. "Cut churn 14% in one quarter" — Name, Title, Company (with logo) beats a wall of anonymous 5-star ratings. Numbers, names, faces. Never fabricate — invented logos, named testimonials, and precision stats ("68% less, median across customers") are a legal and credibility blocker.
- **Trust at zero customers:** the fatal mistake is treating "no fabrication" as "no trust section" — a page with no proof loses to a page with fake proof. Build the honest substitutes: founder/team credibility (who built this and why they're qualified), methodology transparency ("reach × revenue × recency ÷ effort — no black boxes"), verifiable domain facts with sources, security/compliance posture, concrete guarantees and risk reversal, a "built for / not for (yet)" honesty panel, design-partner or beta framing. Label all demo content "sample data".
- **Objection handling / FAQ:** list the top 4–6 real reasons people don't buy (price, effort, switching cost, security, "will it work for my case"), answer honestly. Place the biggest objection's answer directly beside the final CTA.
- **Pricing (if shown):** anchor high, highlight the intended tier, state what happens after the trial. Ambiguity here kills conversion more than price does. Where cost is the audience's main anxiety, never leave the price unargued: pair it with payback math, cost-per-use, a competitor/alternative comparison, or a statutory fee basis. State the full money mechanics wherever a decision happens (deposit, remaining balance, charge timing, refund path).
- **The close — a segmented funnel, not repeated buttons:** end the page with side-by-side path cards, one per funnel, each answering its own audience's objections. Self-serve card: no card required, cancel by doing nothing, what they'll see first. Enterprise/considered card: SSO/SAML, DPA, data residency, migration support, response time. Repeating the same two buttons down the page is not a close; segmenting by readiness is.

## CTA copy

- State the value or the immediate next experience: "Start free — no card required", "See your first roadmap", "Book a 20-min intro call", "Reserve yours — $100 deposit".
- Never: "Submit", "Learn more" (as primary), "Get started" without qualifier.
- Repeat the primary CTA every 1.5–2 viewports; same label every time (changing labels reads as different actions).
- Beside the final CTA: one line of risk reversal ("Cancel anytime", "Deposit fully refundable", "Reply within one business day").

## Voice and editing passes

After drafting, make three passes:

1. **Skim pass:** read only headlines + CTA labels. Must form a complete argument. Fix before anything else.
2. **Spoken pass:** read aloud; anything you wouldn't say to a customer's face gets rewritten ("leverage", "seamless", "empower", "revolutionize", "cutting-edge").
3. **Proof pass:** every claim is either true-and-sourced or deleted. Fabricated metrics and invented testimonials are the one unforgivable sin on a page you're asking strangers to trust.
