# Design Research: Sources, Extraction, and the Direction Memo

## Where to look

Search and fetch from these, filtered to the product's industry plus one adjacent industry (adjacent industries are where the fresh ideas come from):

| Source | What it's good for |
|---|---|
| awwwards.com (Site of the Day / nominees) | Motion language, scroll narratives, art direction with a point of view |
| thefwa.com | Interactive/experimental journeys, WebGL-grade heroes |
| cssdesignawards.com | Balanced design+UX winners |
| godly.website | Curated modern web design, fast to scan |
| land-book.com, lapa.ninja | Volume of real landing pages by category — good for conversion patterns |
| siteinspire.com | Typography-led, editorial direction |
| "best `<industry>` landing pages `<year>`" teardowns | Copy + conversion reasoning, not just looks |

Also fetch the 2–3 strongest direct competitors — not to borrow from, but to know what the visitor has already seen, so the page can deliberately *not* look like that.

## What to extract from each reference (be concrete)

For each of the 3–5 sites you study, note:

- **Hero mechanics:** headline/visual relationship (side-by-side? type over media? product-first?), how many words, where the CTA sits, what moves in the first 2 seconds.
- **Type system:** display vs body faces, size contrast ratio, weight usage, anything unusual (oversized numerals, mixed serif/sans, outlined type).
- **Color strategy:** dominant color and where it's spent; background strategy (sections alternating? one continuous canvas?).
- **Scroll narrative:** list what each viewport-height "chapter" does. This is the most transferable artifact — the skeleton of persuasion.
- **Signature motion:** the ONE animation you'd remember tomorrow, and its trigger (load, scroll-scrub, hover).
- **Product presentation:** raw screenshots, framed, abstracted, interactive demo, video?
- **Social proof treatment:** logos, quotes, numbers — and how they're made credible (attribution, specificity).

## The direction memo (write this before designing)

```
# Direction memo — <project>

## Candidates
### A. "<evocative name>"  (refs: <site1>, <site2>)
- Type: ...
- Color: ...
- Motion grammar: ...
- Hero concept: ...
- Why it fits / why not: ...

### B. "<name>" ...
### C. "<name>" ...   (optional)

## Decision: <A/B/C> because <audience/brand reason, not personal taste>

## Borrowed moves (structural, never verbatim)
- <site>: <specific move> → adapted as <how>
```

## Anti-patterns to detect in your own work

If the emerging design matches 3+ of these, it has collapsed into the generic AI template — go back to the memo:

- Dark page, purple/indigo→blue gradient accents
- 3-column feature grid of glassmorphism cards with icon + heading + two lines
- Emoji as section icons
- Every section: centered heading, centered subtext, centered content
- Inter/Poppins at uniform weights everywhere
- Hero headline of the form "Supercharge/Unlock/Transform your X"
- Fake dashboard screenshot with meaningless charts
